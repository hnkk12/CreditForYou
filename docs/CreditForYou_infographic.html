① Problem diagnosis

The checkout decision is welded to manual underwriting

No payslip · short or no bureau file · income arrives via platforms, e-wallets, online sales
▼
Centralised manual review: documents, CIC check, credit officer
▼
1.8–4.6 business days → the purchase moment is gone
Decline → lost sale for bank & merchant
The review costs more than a small loan earns: a 5M VND, 6-month loan at 12% earns ≈ 276k VND (interest + 2% merchant fee) vs a 380k VND review (Exhibit 2). Review only pays above ≈ 6.9M VND, yet loans start at 1.6M.
Highest leverage: a faster review still costs 380k and keeps customers waiting; collections act only after a "yes". Taking the review out of checkout fixes speed and unit cost at once.

Root cause: income is real but invisible to a document review (stability, 1 − CV)

② Solution concept · data-to-decision architecture

Score cash-flow in advance; the till only checks the limit

1 · Data inputs

What we read

  • 12 months of CASA transactions
  • Active loan payments
  • Age, tenure, segment
  • Basket value at checkout

Phase 2: platform / e-wallet data with consent. No payslip needed.

▶
2 · Logic · nightly batch

How we decide

  • Clean: drop own transfers, refunds, disbursements
  • Income by source, gross→net (0.89 / 0.53)
  • Capacity = min(50% disposable, 20% income, 50% income − debt)
  • Tier A–D + guardrails
  • Limit = min(capacity × annuity, caps)
▶
3 · Outputs

What we produce

  • Limit + status
  • Reason codes
  • Tier & APR band
  • Audit trail
▶
4 · Business actions

What the bank does

  • Till: real-time eKYC & fraud check, then approve / counter-offer / decline
  • Targeting: offers shown only to pre-approved customers
  • 3 on-time → +25%; 30+ DPD → freeze
  • ML: repayment labels train a default model

Output on 500 simulated customers SIMULATED · median limit 15M VND · caps A 90M / B 60M / C 30M / new 30M · tenor 3–12 months

Guardrails only tighten: reject if debt service > 70%, spending > 120% or cash-flow deficit; limit ×0.5 if tier C, debt > 45% or spending > 90%. Salaried with credit (35.5% of mix) keep today's bureau terms via STP; cash-flow adds an affordability flag.
Community impactThin-file workers get a first formal credit line instead of informal credit; repaying grows the limit and a credit history.
Financially sustainableProfitable while default < 4.6% (RoC 28%); stop-rule; rules in a config the credit committee owns.
Environmentally lightPaperless, no documents to scan or courier; one nightly CPU batch on data the bank already holds.

Why ML is not the decision-maker yet: no POP default labels exist. A Lending Club PD model (300k loans, AUC 0.67) mis-ranks tiers (B 2.1% > C 1.9%), so it runs as a challenger until real labels beat the rules.

③ Intervention justification · why it works and pays

3× more gig workers & online merchants served, profitable while default < 4.6%

Reweighted to the Exhibit 2 mix: instant limits for 56% of inbound applications. Gig + MSME n = 43 (95% CI 37%–65%): re-validate in the backtest.
  • Speed & cost: 1.8–4.6 days, 380k/file → instant, ≈ 50k; 199 files skip review.
  • Reach: +211 newly served, −93 over-indebted (mortgage-heavy) declined → net +118.
  • Value: profit 95M VND/yr, RoC 28%; +1 pp merchant fee = +113M VND.
Downside is real, and capped: at ×2 / ×3 default, losses reach 4.3% / 6.5% of volume vs break-even 3.0% (US BNPL charge-off 3.8%). Controls: stop-rule at 4.6% PD, 30M VND starter cap, ×0.5 reduced limits, short tenor, fixed pilot loss budget, pricing lever.
④ Feasibility · 6–12 months, existing customers first
